📊 Executive Overview

Core Innovation

DeepSeek-R1 represents a paradigm shift in training reasoning models by demonstrating that pure reinforcement learning (RL) without supervised fine-tuning (SFT) can incentivize sophisticated reasoning capabilities in LLMs. The model achieves performance comparable to OpenAI's o1-1217 on reasoning benchmarks.

Two-Model Strategy

🔬 DeepSeek-R1-Zero

  • Pure RL approach - No SFT preprocessing
  • Trained directly on DeepSeek-V3-Base
  • Naturally emerges with reasoning behaviors
  • AIME 2024: 15.6% → 71.0% (pass@1)
  • 86.7% with majority voting

🎯 DeepSeek-R1

  • Multi-stage pipeline with cold-start data
  • Addresses readability & language mixing
  • Two RL stages + Two SFT stages
  • Matches OpenAI o1-1217 performance
  • Open-sourced with distilled models

🎓 Core Philosophy & Research Paradigm

Fundamental Thesis

"Reasoning capabilities can emerge purely through reinforcement learning without supervised demonstrations"

This challenges the conventional wisdom that requires extensive supervised fine-tuning with carefully curated chain-of-thought examples before applying RL. DeepSeek-R1 demonstrates that:

  1. Self-Evolution is Possible: LLMs can discover reasoning patterns through exploration and reward signals alone
  2. Emergent Behaviors: Complex capabilities like self-verification, reflection, and long-form reasoning emerge naturally
  3. Inference-Time Scaling: Longer reasoning chains during inference lead to better performance
  4. Distillation Superiority: For smaller models, distilling from larger reasoning models outperforms direct RL training

Philosophical Departures from Conventional Approaches

1. Base Model as Tabula Rasa

Unlike traditional approaches that require SFT to "teach" reasoning patterns, DeepSeek-R1-Zero starts from the base model, treating it as a blank slate for reasoning discovery. This approach:

  • Avoids human bias in reasoning demonstrations
  • Allows the model to explore diverse solution strategies
  • Discovers novel reasoning patterns not present in human data

2. Reward as the Sole Teacher

The model learns entirely from outcome-based rewards (correctness) rather than process supervision. This implies:

  • The model must discover how to reason, not just what to output
  • Reasoning strategies are optimized for effectiveness, not human interpretability
  • The "Aha moment" occurs when the model discovers chain-of-thought as a strategy

3. Multi-Stage Refinement Philosophy

DeepSeek-R1 employs an iterative refinement strategy:

  • Stage 1 RL: Discover raw reasoning capabilities
  • Stage 1 SFT: Refine and make readable through rejection sampling
  • Stage 2 SFT: Integrate with general capabilities (writing, QA)
  • Stage 2 RL: Final alignment across all scenarios
🔑 Key Philosophical Insight: The research demonstrates that reasoning is not a capability that must be explicitly taught through demonstrations, but rather an emergent property that can be incentivized through appropriate reward structures and sufficient exploration.

🚀 SOTA Ideas & Controversial Innovations

1. Pure RL Without SFT Preprocessing

Why This is Controversial:

The community consensus has been that SFT is necessary to provide a "warm start" for RL, especially for complex reasoning tasks. Most prior work (including OpenAI's approach) uses extensive supervised data.

DeepSeek's Innovation:

  • Direct RL on Base Model: Apply GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) directly to DeepSeek-V3-Base
  • Emergent Discovery: The model independently discovers that generating long reasoning chains improves accuracy
  • Self-Verification Emergence: Without being taught, the model learns to verify its own reasoning steps
Community Resistance: Many researchers believe this approach is computationally wasteful and that SFT provides necessary inductive bias. DeepSeek proves otherwise.

2. The "Aha Moment" Phenomenon

Observation:

During RL training, there's a sudden phase transition where the model "discovers" that longer reasoning chains lead to better rewards. This is not gradual but happens abruptly.

Implications:

  • Suggests reasoning capability has a critical threshold
  • Once discovered, the behavior rapidly propagates through the model
  • Challenges smooth optimization assumptions in deep learning theory

Why This Matters:

This phenomenon suggests that reasoning might be a discrete capability rather than a continuous skill, with implications for how we understand and train AI systems.

3. Distillation Superiority Over Direct RL

Controversial Finding:

For smaller models (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B), distilling from DeepSeek-R1 outperforms applying RL directly to the small model.

Implications:

  • Scale-Dependent Discovery: Larger models discover better reasoning patterns
  • Knowledge Transfer: These patterns can be transferred to smaller models via distillation
  • Efficiency Argument: Train one large model with RL, then distill to many small models

Community Debate:

This challenges the notion that each model size should be independently optimized and suggests a hierarchical training strategy.

4. Minimal Cold-Start Data Strategy

Innovation:

DeepSeek-R1 uses only thousands (not millions) of cold-start examples to address readability issues, not to teach reasoning.

Purpose of Cold-Start Data:

  • Format control (not language mixing)
  • Readability improvement
  • Response structure guidance
  • NOT for teaching reasoning patterns

Why This is Different:

Traditional approaches use massive supervised datasets (100K+ examples) to teach reasoning. DeepSeek shows this is unnecessary if RL is properly applied.

5. GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)

Technical Innovation:

Instead of PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization), DeepSeek uses GRPO, which:

  • Compares outputs within a group rather than against a baseline
  • More stable for long-form generation
  • Better suited for reasoning tasks with sparse rewards
  • Reduces variance in gradient estimates

Why Not PPO:

PPO struggles with the high variance of long reasoning chains and the sparse reward signal from correctness-only feedback.

6. Rejection Sampling for SFT Data Creation

Novel Approach:

After RL convergence, DeepSeek uses the RL checkpoint itself to generate SFT data through rejection sampling:

  1. Generate multiple reasoning chains for each problem
  2. Keep only correct solutions
  3. Use these as SFT data for the next stage

Self-Improvement Loop:

This creates a virtuous cycle where the model's own best outputs become training data, continuously raising the quality bar.

7. Two-Stage RL Strategy

Innovation:

Unlike single-stage RL approaches, DeepSeek employs two distinct RL phases:

Stage Focus Data Goal
RL Stage 1 Reasoning Discovery Math, coding, logic problems Maximize reasoning capability
RL Stage 2 General Alignment All scenarios (reasoning + general) Balance reasoning with other capabilities

Why This Matters:

Separating reasoning discovery from general alignment prevents capability dilution and allows focused optimization.

🎯 Meta-Innovation: The entire DeepSeek-R1 approach can be seen as a proof-of-concept that emergence through optimization is a viable alternative to imitation through supervision for complex cognitive capabilities.

🎯 Training Pipeline: Extreme Detail

DeepSeek-R1-Zero Training Process

Step 1: Initialization

  • Starting Point: DeepSeek-V3-Base (no instruction tuning)
  • Initial Capability: AIME 2024 pass@1 = 15.6%
  • Behavior: Generates short, direct answers without reasoning

Step 2: RL Algorithm Setup (GRPO)

Group Relative Policy Optimization:

  • Group Size: Sample K outputs for each prompt (typically K=4-8)
  • Reward Assignment: Binary (correct/incorrect) based on answer verification
  • Advantage Calculation: Compare within group, not against baseline
  • Policy Update: Increase probability of better-performing outputs

Mathematical Formulation:

Advantage_i = Reward_i - mean(Rewards_in_group)
Loss = -E[Advantage * log π_θ(a|s)]

Step 3: Reward Modeling

  • Outcome-Based Rewards: Only final answer correctness matters
  • No Process Supervision: Intermediate steps not evaluated
  • Verification Methods:
    • Math: Symbolic verification or numerical comparison
    • Coding: Test case execution
    • Logic: Answer matching
  • Reward Signal: Binary {0, 1} or {-1, +1}

Step 4: Training Template

Special Tokens for Reasoning:

<think> [reasoning process] </think>
<answer> [final answer] </answer>
  • Purpose: Separate reasoning from answer
  • Reward Application: Only <answer> content is verified
  • Emergence: Model learns to use <think> for exploration

Step 5: The "Aha Moment"

Critical Phase Transition (around step 2000-3000):

  • Before: Short responses, low accuracy
  • Discovery: Model realizes longer reasoning → higher rewards
  • After: Rapid increase in reasoning length and accuracy
  • Emergent Behaviors:
    • Self-verification: "Let me check this..."
    • Reflection: "Wait, that doesn't seem right..."
    • Multi-step planning: "First, let's... Then..."

Step 6: Convergence

  • Training Steps: Thousands of RL iterations
  • Final Performance: AIME 2024 pass@1 = 71.0%
  • Issues:
    • Language mixing (English/Chinese)
    • Poor readability
    • Inconsistent formatting

Stage 1: Cold Start SFT (Detailed)

Purpose: Provide minimal formatting guidance, NOT reasoning instruction

  • Data Size: ~Few thousand examples (orders of magnitude less than traditional SFT)
  • Data Content:
    • Well-formatted reasoning examples
    • Proper use of <think> and <answer> tags
    • Consistent language (no mixing)
    • Clear step-by-step structure
  • Training: Standard supervised learning, few epochs
  • Result: Model learns format, not reasoning strategy

Stage 2: Reasoning-Oriented RL

Identical to R1-Zero RL, but starting from cold-start checkpoint

  • Data: Math, coding, science reasoning problems
  • Algorithm: GRPO with outcome rewards
  • Key Difference: Better initial formatting reduces exploration waste
  • Duration: Until convergence on reasoning benchmarks
  • Monitoring:
    • Reasoning length trends
    • Accuracy on held-out validation
    • Diversity of reasoning strategies

Stage 3: Rejection Sampling

Self-Distillation Process:

  1. Generation: Use RL checkpoint to generate N solutions per problem (N=16-64)
  2. Verification: Check correctness of each solution
  3. Selection: Keep only correct solutions
  4. Filtering: Apply quality criteria:
    • Readability score
    • Reasoning coherence
    • Length appropriateness
  5. Augmentation: Combine with DeepSeek-V3 general data (writing, QA, etc.)

Result: High-quality mixed dataset for next SFT stage

Stage 4: Combined SFT

Purpose: Integrate reasoning with general capabilities

  • Data Mix:
    • ~50% Rejection-sampled reasoning data
    • ~50% DeepSeek-V3 general data (writing, factual QA, self-cognition)
  • Training: Supervised fine-tuning from V3-Base again (fresh start)
  • Goal: Create a balanced model that can reason AND perform general tasks
  • Challenge: Prevent reasoning capability degradation

Stage 5: Full-Scenario RL

Final Alignment Stage:

  • Data: All task types (reasoning + general)
  • Rewards:
    • Reasoning tasks: Correctness
    • General tasks: Human preference (helpfulness, harmlessness)
  • Algorithm: GRPO with multi-objective rewards
  • Balancing: Careful reward weighting to maintain reasoning while improving general performance
  • Result: DeepSeek-R1 final checkpoint

Distillation Process

Teacher Model: DeepSeek-R1

Student Models: Qwen2.5 and Llama3 series (1.5B - 70B)

Distillation Steps:

  1. Data Generation:
    • Use DeepSeek-R1 to generate reasoning chains for diverse prompts
    • Cover math, coding, science, general reasoning
    • Generate ~800K reasoning examples
  2. Data Filtering:
    • Verify correctness
    • Filter by length (remove too short/long)
    • Ensure diversity
  3. Student Training:
    • Standard supervised fine-tuning
    • Student learns to mimic teacher's reasoning patterns
    • Multiple epochs until convergence
  4. Evaluation:
    • Test on reasoning benchmarks
    • Compare against direct RL on student
    • Result: Distillation > Direct RL for small models
🔬 Critical Training Insight: The multi-stage pipeline is not just about improving performance—it's about separating concerns. Each stage has a specific purpose: discovery, refinement, integration, and alignment. This modular approach allows for targeted optimization and easier debugging.

⚡ Inference Strategy & Test-Time Scaling

Core Inference Philosophy

Longer reasoning = Better performance

DeepSeek-R1 is designed to leverage inference-time compute by generating extended reasoning chains. Unlike traditional models that aim for concise outputs, R1 benefits from "thinking longer."

Inference Modes

🎯 Pass@1 (Single Sample)

  • Method: Generate one reasoning chain
  • Temperature: Low (0.6-0.7) for consistency
  • Max Tokens: 8K-32K (allow long reasoning)
  • Use Case: Standard inference, API deployment

🔄 Majority Voting

  • Method: Generate N chains (N=8-64)
  • Temperature: Higher (0.8-1.0) for diversity
  • Aggregation: Vote on final answers
  • Performance Gain: +10-15% accuracy
  • Cost: N× compute

🌳 Best-of-N Sampling

  • Method: Generate N, select best by confidence
  • Selection Criteria:
    • Self-verification passed
    • Reasoning coherence
    • Model confidence score
  • Use Case: High-stakes problems

Inference Optimization Techniques

1. Early Stopping for Efficiency

For simple problems, the model can be configured to stop reasoning early if confidence is high:

  • Monitor self-verification signals
  • If model expresses high confidence early, truncate reasoning
  • Saves compute on easy problems

2. Adaptive Compute Allocation

Dynamically adjust max tokens based on problem difficulty:

  • Easy problems: 2K-4K tokens
  • Medium problems: 8K-16K tokens
  • Hard problems: 32K+ tokens
  • Difficulty estimation: Based on problem type or initial model uncertainty

3. Speculative Reasoning

For majority voting, generate chains in parallel:

  • Leverage batch processing on GPUs
  • Generate 8-16 chains simultaneously
  • Reduces wall-clock time vs. sequential generation

Performance Scaling with Compute

Inference Strategy Compute Cost AIME 2024 Accuracy Latency
Pass@1 (Short) 1× ~60% Low
Pass@1 (Long) 2-3× 71.0% Medium
Majority@8 16-24× ~80% High (parallel)
Majority@64 128-192× 86.7% Very High
⚡ Inference Insight: DeepSeek-R1 demonstrates a clear compute-performance tradeoff at inference time. This is fundamentally different from traditional models where more compute doesn't help after generation. R1 enables "buying" better performance with more inference compute.

🎓 Key Takeaways for PhD Students & Industry Experts

Paradigm Shifts

  1. RL Sufficiency: Supervised fine-tuning is not necessary for reasoning—RL alone can discover complex cognitive patterns
  2. Emergence Over Imitation: Letting models discover reasoning through exploration may be superior to teaching through demonstration
  3. Scale-Dependent Discovery: Larger models discover better reasoning patterns, which can be distilled to smaller models
  4. Inference-Time Scaling: Performance can be "purchased" at inference time through longer reasoning or multiple samples

Implications for Research

  • Rethink SFT: The role of supervised learning in post-training needs reconsideration
  • Reward Design: Simple outcome-based rewards may be sufficient; complex process rewards might be unnecessary
  • Training Efficiency: Multi-stage pipelines with clear separation of concerns enable better optimization
  • Distillation Strategy: For model families, train large with RL, distill to small

Open Questions

  • What is the minimum model size for RL-based reasoning discovery?
  • Can this approach extend to multimodal reasoning?
  • How to make reasoning more interpretable and controllable?
  • Can we predict the "Aha moment" or trigger it earlier?
  • What are the theoretical foundations of emergent reasoning?
🚀 Bottom Line: DeepSeek-R1 proves that reasoning is an emergent capability that can be incentivized through pure reinforcement learning. This challenges fundamental assumptions about how we train AI systems and opens new research directions for the community.
