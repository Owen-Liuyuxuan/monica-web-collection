DRAC · PET · TTC · RSS — Concepts, Formulas & Interactive Comparison

DRAC Deceleration Rate to Avoid Collision

DRAC measures the minimum deceleration required for the ego vehicle (and/or the other vehicle) to avoid a collision. It answers: "Is the required braking physically feasible?" In the context of DRAC + PET, both vehicles are assumed to decelerate cooperatively, and a PET margin is used as the safety buffer.

FORMULA
\[ DRAC = \frac{(v_{ego} - v_{obj})^2}{2 \cdot d_{gap}} \]
where \(v_{ego}\) = ego speed, \(v_{obj}\) = object speed, \(d_{gap}\) = current gap between vehicles

PET Post-Encroachment Time

PET measures the time gap between two vehicles occupying the same conflict zone. It is defined as the time from when the first vehicle clears the conflict point to when the second vehicle arrives at it. A small PET means a near-miss; PET = 0 means a collision.

FORMULA
\[ PET = t_{clear} - t_{arrive} \]
\(t_{clear}\): time when vehicle A clears the conflict zone
\(t_{arrive}\): time when vehicle B enters the conflict zone
PET > 0: near-miss  |  PET ≤ 0: collision
PET as DRAC Margin

In DRAC+PET checks, PET defines the minimum acceptable time gap after avoidance maneuver. Both vehicles decelerate and must achieve at least this temporal separation.

PET as TTC Near-Miss Threshold

In TTC+PET checks, PET defines the near-miss boundary. If TTC is within braking distance and PET is small, the scenario is flagged as high-risk.

TTC Time to Collision

TTC measures how long until a collision occurs if both vehicles maintain their current speeds. In the TTC + PET context, the ego travels at planned speed and checks whether a near-miss (small PET) occurs within the braking distance — i.e., whether the ego could not stop in time.

FORMULA
\[ TTC = \frac{d_{gap}}{v_{ego} - v_{obj}} \quad (v_{ego} > v_{obj}) \]
Braking distance: \(\displaystyle d_{brake} = \frac{v_{ego}^2}{2 \cdot a_{max}}\)
Risk condition: TTC is small AND near-miss occurs within \(d_{brake}\)

RSS Responsibility-Sensitive Safety

RSS (Intel, 2017) defines a formal mathematical model of safe distances. In the collision-checking context: "If an object in the ego's passage area suddenly performs maximum braking, can the ego stop before hitting it?" RSS assumes worst-case behavior of the object — no cooperation assumed.

SAFE LONGITUDINAL DISTANCE
\[ d_{safe} = v_{ego} \cdot \rho + \frac{v_{ego}^2}{2 a_{min,ego}} - \frac{v_{obj}^2}{2 a_{max,obj}} \]
\(\rho\): reaction time  |  \(a_{min,ego}\): ego's min braking  |  \(a_{max,obj}\): object's max braking (worst case)
Unsafe if \(d_{gap} < d_{safe}\)

⚖️ Key Philosophical Differences
DRAC+PET — Cooperative

Assumes both agents act rationally and decelerate. Models the best-case avoidance scenario. If DRAC exceeds comfortable deceleration, even cooperative braking cannot avoid collision.

TTC+PET — Predictive

Assumes no action taken — constant speeds. Models the extrapolated future. Checks whether the planned trajectory leads to a near-miss that cannot be avoided by braking alone.

RSS — Worst-Case Formal

Assumes the object behaves adversarially (max braking). Models the worst-case scenario. Provides a provable safety guarantee — if d_gap ≥ d_safe, ego can always stop.

PET — Observational

Originally a post-hoc traffic safety metric (from video analysis). In AV context, used as a temporal safety margin to define how close a near-miss is acceptable.
